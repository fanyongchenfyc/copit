\begin{abstract}
High-fidelity Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) simulations remain the standard tool for aerodynamic analysis, yet each solve on an unstructured mesh is computationally expensive, and many-query tasks such as design exploration, optimisation and multi-condition evaluation therefore become prohibitive. Neural operators provide mesh-flexible surrogates for the map from geometry and operating conditions to flow fields; in existing architectures, however, global parameters such as the freestream state and design variables are typically concatenated with pointwise inputs, so their influence on deeper geometric representations remains implicit. This paper proposes CoPiT, a \textbf{Co}nditional \textbf{P}osition-induced \textbf{T}ransformer that retains a position-induced Transformer as the geometric backbone and injects a global condition vector by feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM), thereby separating spatial aggregation from parametric response. The model is evaluated on two-dimensional AirfRANS airfoil fields, including scarce-data and extrapolation regimes, and on three-dimensional BlendedNet++ blended-wing-body surfaces. Explicit FiLM conditioning improves over input concatenation, while the preferred placement of FiLM is task-dependent. CoPiT thus provides a practical operator-learning surrogate for parametric aerodynamics on unstructured meshes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% =====================================================================
Parametric partial differential equations (PDEs) are central to fluid mechanics and aerospace applications~\cite{anderson2017fundamentals,vinuesa2022cfd_ml}. In particular, high-fidelity Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) simulations yield velocity, pressure and wall-shear fields that are indispensable for aerodynamic analysis, yet each solve is computationally expensive. Many-query tasks---design exploration, optimisation and multi-condition evaluation---therefore motivate surrogate models that approximate the map from geometry and freestream or design parameters to aerodynamic fields at a fraction of the CFD cost~\cite{brunton2020ml_fluid,karniadakis2021piml}. Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate_pipeline} illustrates this surrogate-assisted workflow.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,keepaspectratio]{figs/fig1_v2.png}
\caption{Schematic of a surrogate-assisted aerodynamic design workflow. Offline CFD generates training data; a learned operator then provides approximate evaluations during design exploration. Latency and uncertainty annotations in the figure are illustrative and are not measurements reported in this study.}
\label{fig:surrogate_pipeline}
\end{figure}

Neural operators learn mappings between function spaces and, once trained, evaluate new inputs by a single forward pass~\cite{li2021fno,lu2021deeponet,kovachki2023neural}. Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) and DeepONet are standard constructions on structured domains~\cite{li2021fno,lu2021deeponet}; Geo-FNO and GINO extend related ideas to more general geometries~\cite{li2023geofno,li2022gino}. Graph networks and transformer-based solvers further address unstructured meshes~\cite{pfaff2021learning,wu2024transolver,luo2025transolverpp,chen2024pit}. These developments make operator learning a natural framework for aerodynamic field prediction, where the surrogate must remain accurate across varying meshes and parametric regimes.

Aerodynamic data differ from many PDE benchmarks in two respects. First, the discrete domain is geometry-dependent (unstructured meshes or surface triangulations) rather than a fixed Cartesian grid. Second, the solution depends jointly on local geometry and global parameters such as freestream speed, angle of attack and design variables~\cite{bhatnagar2019cnn_aero,thuerey2020deepcfd,bonnet2022airfrans,sung2025blendednetpp}. Geometry-aware architectures (MeshGraphNets, Transolver, PiT) capture spatial interactions effectively~\cite{pfaff2021learning,wu2024transolver,chen2024pit}, but freestream and design parameters are often supplied only by input concatenation and then processed implicitly by the backbone. Such one-shot fusion does not explicitly distinguish geometry-driven aggregation from parametric response modulation. Whether repeated conditioning improves field or integrated-load prediction is therefore an empirical question addressed in this work.

Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) offers a lightweight alternative: a small network maps a parameter vector to channel-wise scale and shift coefficients that modulate intermediate features~\cite{perez2018film,dumoulin2018film_survey}. Originally developed for visual reasoning, FiLM is adopted here as an explicit, multi-stage conditioning mechanism inside a mesh-based operator.

We propose \emph{CoPiT}, a \textbf{Co}nditional \textbf{P}osition-induced \textbf{T}ransformer for aerodynamic field prediction. CoPiT retains PiT---position-induced attention with a latent-mesh encode--process--decode structure~\cite{chen2024pit}---as the geometric backbone, and injects multi-stage FiLM conditioning after instance normalisation in every processor block and in the decoder. On both AirfRANS and the reported BlendedNet++ model, global operating or design parameters enter through FiLM and are not concatenated into the geometric encoder features. We evaluate CoPiT on AirfRANS~\cite{bonnet2022airfrans} (two-dimensional RANS airfoil fields; full, scarce and extrapolation splits) and on BlendedNet++~\cite{sung2025blendednetpp} (three-dimensional blended-wing-body surface fields).

The contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em,topsep=0.3em]
\item \textbf{Architecture.} We propose CoPiT, which couples a PiT geometric backbone with multi-stage FiLM. Processor-plus-decoder FiLM is the default configuration reported throughout; matched ablations show that the preferred FiLM placement is task- and metric-dependent.
\item \textbf{Kernel preservation.} We show that pointwise FiLM does not modify the positional-attention weights and preserves convergence of an already convergent attention output under bounded modulation. This observation concerns the kernel itself and is not a discretisation-consistency proof for the complete implementation.
\item \textbf{Evaluation.} We assess the architecture on two- and three-dimensional aerodynamic datasets, including scarce-data and extrapolation settings, field quantities and integrated-load rankings. Matched conditioning ablations on AirfRANS and BlendedNet++ show that FiLM generally outperforms input concatenation, while decoder-only FiLM can outperform complete CoPiT on AirfRANS field error.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} states the learning problem and reviews position-induced attention. Section~\ref{sec:method} introduces CoPiT. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports numerical experiments. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
%% =====================================================================
Neural operators approximate solution maps of parametric PDEs from data~\cite{kovachki2023neural}. DeepONet represents the operator as a branch--trunk product~\cite{lu2021deeponet}; Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) mix features in the frequency domain on regular grids~\cite{li2021fno}. Geo-FNO and GINO extend related ideas to deformed or unstructured geometries~\cite{li2023geofno,li2022gino}. Graph networks such as MeshGraphNets operate directly on mesh connectivity~\cite{pfaff2021learning}. Transformer-based solvers, including Transolver and Transolver++~\cite{wu2024transolver,luo2025transolverpp}, and the Position-induced Transformer (PiT)~\cite{chen2024pit}, address general point clouds by replacing or restricting content-based attention. PiT in particular uses pairwise distances to form the attention kernel, which is the geometric backbone adopted here.

Conditioning on global parameters is often implemented by concatenating a broadcast vector to pointwise features. GNOT treats multiple input functions through a linear-attention encoder~\cite{hao2023gnot}. Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) instead maps a condition vector to channel-wise scale and shift~\cite{perez2018film,dumoulin2018film_survey}; FiLMNet uses this mechanism without a position-induced backbone on BlendedNet++~\cite{sung2025blendednetpp}. CoPiT combines PiT's geometric attention with multi-stage FiLM so that spatial aggregation and parametric modulation remain distinct.

Data-driven aerodynamic surrogates range from convolutional predictors of airfoil fields~\cite{bhatnagar2019cnn_aero,thuerey2020deepcfd} to operator models trained on AirfRANS~\cite{bonnet2022airfrans} and on three-dimensional blended-wing-body surfaces in BlendedNet++~\cite{sung2025blendednetpp}. The present work is evaluated on both of these public corpora.

\subsection{Operator learning}
\label{sec:op_learn}
Consider a parametric PDE of the form
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{a}u = f,
  \label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  a \in \mathcal{A}(\Omega_{a};\mathbb{R}^{d_{a}}),
  \qquad
  u \in \mathcal{U}(\Omega_{u};\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}),
  \qquad
  f \in \mathcal{F}.
  \label{eq:spaces}
\end{equation}
Here $\Omega_{a},\Omega_{u}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$ are bounded domains (not necessarily identical), $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{U}$ are Banach spaces of functions over $\Omega_{a}$ and $\Omega_{u}$, respectively, and $\mathcal{L}_{a}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{F}$ is a differential operator depending on the parameter function $a$. The associated solution operator is
\begin{equation}
  \Phi : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{U},
  \qquad
  u = \Phi(a).
  \label{eq:solution_op}
\end{equation}
Operator learning aims to construct a parameterised surrogate $\Phi_{\theta}$ such that $\Phi_{\theta}(a)\approx\Phi(a)$ for previously unseen inputs $a$~\cite{kovachki2023neural,li2021fno,chen2024pit}.

In applications, $a$ and $u$ are observed only through samples on finite meshes
\begin{equation}
  X_{a}
  =
  \{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{a}}
  \subset
  \Omega_{a},
  \qquad
  X_{u}
  =
  \{\hat{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{u}}
  \subset
  \Omega_{u}.
  \label{eq:meshes}
\end{equation}
Given training pairs $\{(a^{j},u^{j})\}_{j=1}^{J}$, with $a^{j}$ sampled on $X_{a}$ and $u^{j}$ on $X_{u}$, the parameters $\theta$ are obtained by minimising
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\theta}\;
  \frac{1}{J}
  \sum_{j=1}^{J}
  \bigl\|
    u^{j}
    -
    \Phi_{\theta}(a^{j})
  \bigr\|^{2},
  \label{eq:emp_risk}
\end{equation}
where the residual is evaluated on the output mesh (or on a subsample of it). After training, $\Phi_{\theta}$ is expected to accept new sampling locations of $a$ and to predict $u$ at arbitrary query locations in $\Omega_{u}$.

\noindent\textbf{Aerodynamic setting.}\ 
In the present work the parameter function is induced by a geometry $g$ and a global parameter vector $\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{c}}$ collecting freestream and design quantities. We write $a=a_{g,\mathbf{c}}$ and allow $\Omega_{a}$ and $\Omega_{u}$ to depend on $g$. The output fields considered later are
\begin{align}
  u_{g,\mathbf{c}}(x)
  &=
  \bigl(v_{x}(x),\, v_{y}(x),\, p(x),\, \nu_{t}(x)\bigr)
  &&
  \text{(AirfRANS)},
  \label{eq:airfrans_target}\\
  u_{g,\mathbf{c}}(x)
  &=
  \bigl(C_{p}(x),\, C_{f,x}(x),\, C_{f,z}(x)\bigr)
  &&
  \text{(BlendedNet++)},
  \label{eq:bwb_target}
\end{align}
and the learning task is to approximate $u_{g,\mathbf{c}}$ by $\Phi_{\theta}(a_{g,\mathbf{c}})$ under the corresponding data protocol.

\subsection{Position-induced attention}
\label{sec:posatt}
A standard building block of Transformer architectures is content-based self-attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention}. Query, key and value projections of a feature matrix are combined through a softmax over pairwise feature affinities, so the weights depend on the current field values, must be recomputed for every input, and do not directly encode the geometry of the underlying mesh. For PDE surrogates this is unnatural: the relative positions of sampling points largely determine how local information should be aggregated.

Position-induced attention incorporates such geometric structure in an explicit and efficient way~\cite{chen2024pit}. Let $U\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{v}\times d_{\ell}}$ collect the values of a feature field on a mesh $X_{v}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{v}}$, and define the pairwise squared-distance matrix
\begin{equation}
  D_{ij}
  =
  \|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{2}^{2},
  \qquad
  i,j=1,\ldots,N_{v}.
  \label{eq:dist_mat}
\end{equation}
With a positive bandwidth $\lambda>0$ and a value matrix $W^{V}$, the basic position-attention operator is
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{PosAtt}(U;D)
  =
  \operatorname{Softmax}(-\lambda D)\,
  U W^{V}.
  \label{eq:posatt}
\end{equation}
Each row of the output is therefore a distance-weighted average of value-transformed features: the kernel depends on mesh geometry rather than on the current feature content. Multi-head variants employ head-wise scales $\lambda_{h}$, implemented through a positive reparameterisation $\phi(\lambda_{h})$. When the query and key meshes differ, the same construction yields \emph{cross} position-attention and realises a learnable change of resolution; when they coincide, one obtains \emph{self} position-attention on a single mesh. A locality parameter $\tau\in(0,1]$ may further restrict attention to a nearest-neighbour fraction of keys (quantile masking), while $\tau=1$ recovers a global kernel.

A central property of~\eqref{eq:posatt} is discretisation consistency: as the underlying mesh is refined, the discrete operator converges in probability to an integral operator with a normalised Gaussian-type kernel. A precise statement and proof are given as Theorem~2.1 in~\cite{chen2024pit}. This property makes position-attention an attractive geometric primitive for mesh-based aerodynamic surrogates.

In the PiT architecture~\cite{chen2024pit}, position-attention is organised in an encoder--processor--decoder form with a latent mesh of moderate size. Cross position-attention maps the input mesh to the latent mesh and, later, the latent representation to the output mesh, while a stack of self position-attention blocks refines features on the latent mesh. The next section builds CoPiT on this geometric organisation.

\section{CoPiT: conditional position-induced Transformer}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}
The architecture is summarised in Fig.~\ref{fig:copit_arch}. Given an input mesh with geometric features, a target mesh and a condition vector $\mathbf{c}$, the network (i)~encodes the input onto a latent mesh, (ii)~refines latent features with $L$ processor blocks, each followed by FiLM, and (iii)~decodes to the target mesh with a final FiLM stage before the readout. The same skeleton is used for AirfRANS and BlendedNet++; dataset-specific inputs, the content of $\mathbf{c}$ and auxiliary decoder channels are stated in Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\linewidth,keepaspectratio]{figs/copit_v2.png}
\caption{Overview of CoPiT. An encoder maps the input mesh to a latent mesh; $L$ processor blocks apply position-attention, instance normalisation and FiLM; a decoder with a further FiLM stage reads out the predicted field. A FiLM generator maps the global condition $\mathbf{c}$ to channel-wise modulation parameters.}
\label{fig:copit_arch}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Multi-stage FiLM conditioning}
\label{sec:film}
Let $\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ be a hidden feature at a mesh (or latent) point after instance normalisation~\cite{ulyanov2016instance}. A FiLM generator $g_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{c}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2D}$ maps $\mathbf{c}$ to scale and shift vectors $(\gamma(\mathbf{c}),\beta(\mathbf{c}))$ and applies
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mathbf{h}}
  =
  \gamma(\mathbf{c})\odot\mathbf{h}+\beta(\mathbf{c}).
  \label{eq:film}
\end{equation}
\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth,keepaspectratio]{figs/FiLM_diagram.pdf}
\caption{Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)~\cite{perez2018film}. A global condition vector $\mathbf{c}$ is mapped to channel-wise scale $\gamma(\mathbf{c})$ and shift $\beta(\mathbf{c})$, which modulate instance-normalised features.}
\label{fig:film}
\end{figure}
Figure~\ref{fig:film} illustrates the mechanism~\cite{perez2018film}. CoPiT uses two generators:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Processor FiLM}: outputs $\{(\gamma_{\ell},\beta_{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{L}$ for all processor blocks (tensor size proportional to $L(1+H)D_{\mathrm{hid}}$);
\item \emph{Decoder FiLM}: outputs $(\gamma_{\mathrm{dec}},\beta_{\mathrm{dec}})$ for the decoder feature map.
\end{itemize}
Each generator is a small MLP (one hidden layer of width $64$ in our experiments) trained end-to-end. Conditions therefore modulate every processing depth rather than only the input layer.

Equation~\eqref{eq:film} implies the following elementary properties of affine channel-wise modulation; the approximation statement is a standard consequence of universal approximation for multilayer perceptrons~\cite{hornik1989mlp}.

\begin{proposition}[Basic properties of FiLM]
\label{prop:film}
Let $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}=\gamma(\mathbf{c})\odot\mathbf{h}+\beta(\mathbf{c})$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\gamma\equiv\mathbf{1}$ and $\beta\equiv\mathbf{0}$ recover the unmodulated feature;
\item[(ii)] $\gamma_{k}=\beta_{k}=0$ suppresses channel $k$;
\item[(iii)] on any compact set of conditions, continuous maps $\mathbf{c}\mapsto(\gamma(\mathbf{c}),\beta(\mathbf{c}))$ can be approximated uniformly by a feed-forward generator of sufficient width~\cite{hornik1989mlp}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Processor and decoder with FiLM}
\label{sec:blocks}
Denote latent features after encoding by $\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ltt}}^{(0)}$. Processor block $\ell=1,\ldots,L$ implements
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{h}_{\ell}
  &=
  \mathrm{PosAtt}\bigl(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{ltt}},\,\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ltt}}^{(\ell-1)}\bigr),
  \label{eq:proc1}\\
  \bar{\mathbf{h}}_{\ell}
  &=
  \mathrm{InstanceNorm}(\mathbf{h}_{\ell}),
  \label{eq:proc2}\\
  \tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{\ell}
  &=
  \gamma_{\ell}(\mathbf{c})\odot\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell}(\mathbf{c}),
  \label{eq:proc3}\\
  \mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ltt}}^{(\ell)}
  &=
  \mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ltt}}^{(\ell-1)}
  +
  \mathrm{GELU}\bigl(\mathrm{MLP}_{\ell}(\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{\ell})\bigr).
  \label{eq:proc4}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:proc3} is the only difference from an unconditioned PiT processor block. The decoder applies position-based cross-attention from $\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{ltt}}$ to $\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{out}}$, optionally merges auxiliary channels $\mathbf{a}$ (in particular the signed distance to the wall), then modulates and reads out:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{g}
  &=
  \mathrm{PosAttCross}\bigl(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{out}},\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{ltt}},\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ltt}}^{(L)}\bigr),
  \label{eq:dec1}\\
  \mathbf{g}
  &\leftarrow
  \mathrm{MLP}_{\mathrm{merge}}\bigl([\mathbf{g}\|\mathrm{MLP}_{\mathrm{aux}}(\mathbf{a})]\bigr),
  \label{eq:dec2}\\
  \mathbf{g}
  &\leftarrow
  \gamma_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbf{c})\odot\mathrm{InstanceNorm}(\mathbf{g})+\beta_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbf{c}),
  \label{eq:dec3}\\
  \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{out}}
  &=
  \mathrm{MLP}_{\mathrm{de}}(\mathbf{g}).
  \label{eq:dec4}
\end{align}
Linear layers use Kaiming initialisation~\cite{he2016resnet} and GELU activations. Residual connections in~\eqref{eq:proc4} stabilise deep processors.

\subsection{Preservation of the positional kernel under FiLM}
\label{sec:consistency}
The position-attention layer analysed in PiT converges under mesh refinement to a kernel integral operator under the assumptions stated in~\cite{chen2024pit}. FiLM~\eqref{eq:film} is a pointwise map and does not alter the distance-based attention weights. The following elementary estimate records this fact. Let $\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{n}$ denote a discrete positional-attention operator on mesh $X_n$, let $\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}$ denote its limiting operator, and let $\Phi_{\mathbf{c}}(z)=\gamma(\mathbf{c})\odot z+\beta(\mathbf{c})$ for a fixed condition $\mathbf{c}$. If $\|\gamma(\mathbf{c})\|_{\infty}<\infty$, then
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\|\Phi_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{n}(U^{n}))
        -\Phi_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(U))\bigr\|
  \le
  \|\gamma(\mathbf{c})\|_{\infty}\,
  \bigl\|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{n}(U^{n})-\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(U)\bigr\|.
  \label{eq:composed_convergence}
\end{equation}
Consequently, bounded FiLM preserves convergence of an already convergent attention output at fixed $\mathbf{c}$. This is a layer-level observation: it does not constitute a complete discretisation-consistency proof for the implemented network, which also contains instance normalisation, nonlinear multilayer perceptrons, latent-mesh selection and auxiliary decoder features. Establishing such a result for the complete composition would require additional assumptions on these components.

\section{Numerical experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
We evaluate CoPiT on AirfRANS and BlendedNet++ and compare it with graph- and transformer-based baselines. Quantities obtained on training data are treated only as optimisation diagnostics; all generalisation claims are based on held-out data.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsubsection{Datasets and metrics}
\textbf{AirfRANS}~\cite{bonnet2022airfrans} provides two-dimensional RANS simulations around NACA-family airfoils under freestream states $(U_{\infty},\alpha)$, with unstructured meshes of order $1.8\times 10^{5}$ points. We consider full data (800/200 train/test), scarce data (200/200), Reynolds-number extrapolation and angle-of-attack extrapolation. The encoder receives four geometric channels (coordinates and unit normals); $\mathbf{c}=(U_{\infty},\alpha)$ enters only through the FiLM generators. The network predicts $(v_{x},v_{y},p,\nu_{t})$ on the CFD mesh. The decoder is additionally conditioned on the signed distance to the wall (SDF) via a small auxiliary MLP, as in~\eqref{eq:dec2}. Held-out field errors are computed in the normalised space. The volume loss is the sum of the pointwise MSEs of $(v_x,v_y,p,\nu_t)$, averaged first over volume points of each test case and then over test cases. Because no-slip velocity and turbulent viscosity are prescribed at the wall in this benchmark, the reported surface loss is the corresponding wall-pressure MSE. Force coefficients are obtained by surface integration of pressure and wall shear using the same sampling, gradient and quadrature implementation for every model. We report Spearman correlations of drag and lift, denoted by $\rho_d$ and $\rho_l$; absolute and relative load errors are discussed separately.

\textbf{BlendedNet++}~\cite{sung2025blendednetpp} is a large-scale blended-wing-body (BWB) surface-flow benchmark. We adopt the official forward split with 8\,992 / 1\,000 / 2\,498 train/validation/test cases and predict surface $(C_{p},C_{f,x},C_{f,z})$. Pointwise encoder inputs are six geometric channels (coordinates and unit normals); the 12-dimensional design/flight vector $\mathbf{c}$ drives processor and decoder FiLM only. Pairwise distances are Euclidean squared distances in ambient $\mathbb{R}^{3}$. The latent mesh retains 10\% of the surface points by Z-order sampling. On this dataset the FiLM scale is implemented as $1+\gamma$, so that a vanishing generator output recovers the identity; AirfRANS uses~\eqref{eq:film} directly. A hybrid variant that additionally broadcasts $\mathbf{c}$ into the pointwise encoding is evaluated only as an ablation (Section~\ref{sec:blended}). Targets are $z$-score normalised using training-set statistics only. Training uses 8\,000 randomly sampled surface points per case per step; test metrics are computed on the full surface as the mean MSE over the three channels, with a per-channel breakdown in the same normalised space.

\subsubsection{Implementation}
On AirfRANS, the latent mesh has 7\,000 points, four processor blocks, hidden width 256 and one attention head. Encoder and decoder locality are $\tau=0.01$; processor self-attention uses $\tau=1$. All three stages employ the same quantile-masked position-induced attention as PiT. Training minimises a surface-weighted mean-squared error with Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam}, initial learning rate $10^{-3}$ and cosine annealing~\cite{loshchilov2017cosine} for 398 epochs. The reported AirfRANS scores use the last-epoch checkpoint: the training script writes a single \texttt{model.pth} at the end of the run. On BlendedNet++, we use hidden width 256, one attention head, four blocks, encoder/decoder locality $\tau=0.01$, AdamW with learning rate $5\times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$, a one-cycle schedule and gradient clipping at unit norm, for 100 epochs. For BlendedNet++, the checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is evaluated once on the test set. Unless stated otherwise, scores are single-run point estimates.

\subsubsection{Baselines}
AirfRANS baselines are MLP~\cite{goodfellow2016deep}, GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017inductive}, PointNet~\cite{qi2017pointnet}, Graph U-Net~\cite{yue2018graph} and Transolver~\cite{wu2024transolver}. On the full-data split we further evaluate matched PiT variants that change only how $(U_{\infty},\alpha)$ is injected: no global condition, input concatenation, processor-only FiLM, decoder-only FiLM, a width-matched concatenation model, and complete CoPiT. BlendedNet++ baselines are reimplemented using the same split, training-set normalisation and surface-MSE definition: PointNet, GraphSAGE, Graph U-Net, GNOT~\cite{hao2023gnot}, Transolver and FiLMNet (FiLM without position-induced attention). On BlendedNet++ we additionally compare concatenation-only and hybrid (broadcast globals plus FiLM) variants of the same PiT backbone.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\noindent\textbf{Ranking versus absolute error.}\ 
The drag coefficient is a small residual between pressure and friction contributions, so relative error is sensitive to modest absolute deviations and to cases with small reference drag. Spearman correlation measures the ordering of cases and can be informative for design screening, but it does not measure calibration or absolute load accuracy. The full-data run combines high $\rho_d$ with a mean relative drag error of approximately $46\%$; both aspects must therefore be reported.

\noindent\textbf{Role of FiLM.}\ 
On AirfRANS (Table~\ref{tab:ablate_air}), a global condition is necessary: geometry-only PiT is not competitive. FiLM variants improve surface loss and $\rho_d$ relative to concatenation; increasing hidden width without FiLM does not close that gap. Decoder-only FiLM can outperform complete CoPiT on field MSE, so the two FiLM stages are not uniformly additive, while complete CoPiT attains the highest $\rho_d$ in the reported family. On BlendedNet++ (Table~\ref{tab:ablate_bnpp}), FiLM-only CoPiT outperforms both concatenation and hybrid broadcast-plus-FiLM on the same backbone and Euclidean metric.

\noindent\textbf{Complexity.}\ 
If $N_{\mathrm{ltt}}$ denotes the latent-mesh size, $L$ the number of processor blocks, $H$ the number of heads and $D$ the hidden width, dense processor self-attention scales as $\mathcal{O}(L N_{\mathrm{ltt}}^{2} H D)$ in arithmetic operations and requires quadratic attention storage unless evaluated in chunks. Encoder and decoder costs depend on the number of retained query--key pairs and are linear in the full-mesh size only when the neighbourhood size is bounded or chunking avoids materialising a dense matrix. Wall-clock training times on one NVIDIA A100 (80\,GB) for the main single-run checkpoints are listed in Table~\ref{tab:runtime}. CoPiT is smaller than Transolver on AirfRANS (Table~\ref{tab:params}) and trains in less wall-clock time on that dataset; on BlendedNet++ the two training times are comparable.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}\ 
Proximity-based attention may still couple points that are close in ambient space but separated on the surface; topology-aware metrics are left to future work. Near-wall residuals dominate the field errors. All principal comparisons use one random seed. The present training is fully supervised (surface-weighted or plain MSE); residual-based regularisers and tighter coupling to design optimisation are left for future work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% =====================================================================
We have presented CoPiT, a conditional neural operator for aerodynamic field prediction on irregular meshes. Building on a position-induced Transformer backbone, CoPiT injects multi-stage FiLM conditioning from freestream and design parameters in the processor and decoder. The pointwise modulation leaves positional-attention weights unchanged, although a discretisation-consistency result for the complete implementation would require additional analysis. On the AirfRANS full-data test set, CoPiT has the lowest surface and volume losses among the compared published baselines and a higher drag rank correlation than Transolver. A matched ablation shows that an unconditioned PiT fails on the fields, concatenation is weaker than FiLM, and decoder-only FiLM can attain lower field losses than complete CoPiT. Advantages are not uniform across the more difficult settings: Graph U-Net has the lowest scarce-data volume loss, and Transolver is more accurate on angle-of-attack extrapolation fields and lift ranking. The large relative drag errors further show that rank correlation alone is insufficient to establish load accuracy. On BlendedNet++, CoPiT obtains the lowest overall normalised test-MSE point estimate among the reimplemented baselines, and a matched ablation favours FiLM-only conditioning over concatenation and hybrid broadcast-plus-FiLM. All scores are single-run point estimates. Future work includes multi-seed evaluation, physics-aware load regularisation, topology-aware metrics, adaptive latent meshes and coupling the surrogate to aerodynamic optimisation loops.
